The Science Behind It

From maize research
to predictive tooling

CrossWise emerged from a study of maize — a staple plant in global food systems. We built a tool that makes nucleotide-level hybrid prediction fast and accessible.

Why hybrid prediction matters

Hybrid breeding is one of the most powerful tools in modern agriculture. By crossing two genetically distinct parent plants, breeders can produce offspring — hybrids — that outperform either parent in traits like yield, drought tolerance, and disease resistance. This phenomenon is called heterosis, or hybrid vigour.

While decades of genetic data make heterosis prediction for maize tractable in theory, modern breeding programs often still rely on expensive and time-consuming field trials. We built a tool specifically tailored to maize to make these predictions faster, cheaper, and more accessible.

Our approach

CrossWise uses a multi-trait SNP genotyping approach: instead of requiring full genomic data, users provide SNP markers for each parent. The model generates thousands of virtual parent pairings and predicts 5 key traits across various environments.

These eight values (four per parent) feed into a trained ML model that predicts the most likely hybrid cultivar class, GC content of the offspring, estimated SNP count, yield potential in kg/ha, and resistance percentages for drought and disease.

The model was trained on a curated dataset of known parent-offspring pairings from published maize hybridisation studies. The predictions are probabilistic estimates — intended to assist early-stage screening, not replace field trials.

What's next

We're expanding the training dataset, adding multi-parent cross support, and building export pipelines directly compatible with field trial management software. If you're a researcher working with maize, we'd love to hear from you.
